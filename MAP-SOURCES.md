# Map sources

Country outlines in `MapData.js` come from [Natural Earth 1:50m Admin 0 countries](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-cultural-vectors/50m-admin-0-countries-2/), retrieved on 2026-09-16 from the [Natural Earth vector repository](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_50m_admin_0_countries.geojson). Natural Earth data is [public domain](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/).

The bundled subset includes Norway, Sweden, Finland and Denmark, plus neighboring Germany for context. Coordinates are rounded to four decimal places; only exterior rings are retained (the four covered countries keep every island's outline, since they are all part of the forecast coverage area; Germany keeps only its largest outline for context). Norway's outline excludes its remote Arctic territories (Svalbard and Jan Mayen), which sit outside MET Norway's Nowcast coverage area. City markers are approximate city centers for navigation, not address lookup results — the forecast is always fetched for the exact coordinates picked, never snapped to a city. The map is a regional overview, not a precise coverage boundary or a live rain overlay.

The `radarCoverage` polygon in `MapData.js` (shaded red on the map, outside it) comes from MET Norway's own published Nowcast coverage shapefile at `https://api.met.no/weatherapi/nowcast/2.0/coverage`, retrieved 2026-09-16, under the same [CC BY 4.0](https://api.met.no/doc/TermsOfService) terms as the forecast API. The original polygon (8231 points) was simplified with Douglas-Peucker (epsilon 0.008°) to 75 points; this only visibly changes the boundary at a much closer zoom than this map ever renders at. Note this shapefile is the *nominal* outer edge of the product's coverage area — it does not encode small-scale radar dead zones from local terrain (mountains, fjords), which MET Norway's API can only report per-point, live, for the exact coordinates queried (via the response's `radar_coverage` field). Within the bundled Nordic countries, the polygon excludes almost no real territory; the visible red area is mostly the corner beyond Finland/Norway's eastern border.
